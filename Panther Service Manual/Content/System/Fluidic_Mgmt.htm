Instrument Fluidic Management

The instrument incorporates the following items to ensure controlled fluid management:

  • Dual pipettor arm with air based pipetting—utilizes both capacitive and pressure based fluid detection (LLD) and pressure based dispense verification (RDV).
  • A precision dry syringe pump is used to accurately aspirate and dispense volumes from 25 to 1000 µL.
  • Built into the pump between the syringe and the pipette tip is a pressure transducer that records the pressure waveform when dispensing and/or aspiration occurs. Characteristics of the curve are used to verify dispense/aspiration process (RDV).
  • The conductive pipette tip is attached to the syringe pump through a stainless steel interface that conducts an oscillating current, used to measure changes in capacitance. When the pipette tip touches fluid, the capacitance changes and can be detected through the liquid level detection (LLD) circuitry.
  • The fluid levels in the reagent bottles are detected with LLD, and the volume is calculated based on the known bottle geometries.
  • The instrument uses precision Auto Detect pumps to ensure accurate Luminometer light off characteristics.
  • The Auto Detect pumps has a home sensors to ensure accurate dispensing. Addition of detect reagents is verified through bubble sensors and a light off algorithm.
  • The instrument includes Magnetic Wash, oil, and DQ pumps to dispense their respective fluids.
  • Verification of wash buffer addition and aspiration (residual volumes) is conducted by means of LLD by the Magnetic Wash aspirator probes. Oil is verified with an in-line pressure sensor.
  • All system fluid bottles are located in a drawer under the instrument deck.
  • RFID is used to manage the inventory of the universal system fluid bottles.
  • Connections to bottles use quick release fittings and are color coordinated.
  • Waste fluid is aspirated using a Vacuum Pump.
  • A 0.2 micron PTFE sterile air filter ensures that the air flowing out of the Vacuum Pump and into the room is free of biohazard waste.
  • A pressure transducer is used to monitor the vacuum pressure at the manifold to ensure that the system is functioning within its operational limits.
  • Pump speed control is used to maintain a constant vacuum pressure at the manifold independent of elevation or system wear.
  • The waste bottle has empty and full sensors.
